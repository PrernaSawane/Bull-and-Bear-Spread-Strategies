💡 Why Does the Bear Put Spread Have Superior Reward-to-Risk (2.18:1 vs 1.51:1)?

Under the Black-Scholes-Merton model, the risk-neutral forward price is $F = S_0 e^{rT} = \$100 \times e^{0.05 \times 0.5} = \$102.53$. Because the forward price drifts upward with the positive risk-free rate, the long call at $K=100$ is effectively in-the-money relative to forward price ($C_1 = \$6.89$). Conversely, the long put at $K=100$ is out-of-the-money relative to the forward price ($P_2 = \$4.42$). This structural time-value discount makes the Bear Put debit spread cost only $3.14 vs $3.98, unlocking a significantly higher profit ceiling ($6.86 vs $6.02) across the same $10 spread width.
